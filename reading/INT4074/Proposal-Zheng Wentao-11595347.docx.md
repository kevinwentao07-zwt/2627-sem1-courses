ICT Learning Companion
 (Zheng 
Wentao
 1159534)
ICT Learning Companion is a mobile application designed to help secondary school students learn and revise ICT in a more organised, visual and personalised way. Instead of working only as a question bank or a study planner, the app combines learning materials, interactive visualisation, practice, performance analysis and AI-supported study planning in one platform. Each ICT topic, such as computer hardware, networking, databases, programming and cybersecurity, begins with a short selected YouTube video so that students can first build a basic understanding of the topic. After that, the app provides visual learning activities to make abstract concepts easier to understand. For example, in a hardware topic, students may explore an interactive 3D computer model and view components such as the CPU, RAM and storage devices separately. In programming topics, the app can show code running step by step, including changes in variables, loops and errors, so that students can follow the logic instead of only looking at the final output.
After learning a topic, students complete short practice activities. Their answers are recorded according to topic and subtopic, allowing the system to identify weak areas and repeated mistakes rather than only giving an overall score. The app then combines this performance data with information such as the student's examination date and available study time. AI is used to turn this information into a personalised study plan, giving more attention to weaker topics while reducing unnecessary revision of areas the student already understands. As the student continues to practise, new results can be used to adjust future learning priorities, creating a continuous learning cycle.
Several existing educational applications already provide parts of this experience. Quizlet is effective for revision because it offers flashcards, practice questions and study materials across many subjects. Its main strength is flexibility and active recall, but as a general-purpose learning tool, it does not focus strongly on ICT-specific visual learning such as 3D hardware models or visual programming execution. Brilliant, on the other hand, is strong in interactive and visual learning, especially in mathematics, programming and other STEM subjects. Its step-by-step activities help learners understand concepts, but its content is not designed around a complete school ICT curriculum. MyStudyLife focuses mainly on academic organisation by helping students manage classes, assignments, examinations and revision schedules. This is useful for time management, but its planning is mainly based on deadlines and tasks rather than students' detailed learning performance.
The main advantage of ICT Learning Companion is therefore not one individual feature, but the way these functions are connected. Students first learn through videos and visualisation, then practise what they have learned, receive analysis of their weaknesses, and finally get a study plan based on their actual performance. By combining ICT-specific visual learning, performance analysis and personalised planning, the app aims to make revision more focused, efficient and responsive to each student's progress.
