## 第 2 页

1. The app in brief
ICT Learning Companion is an e-learning app for Hong Kong S4–S6 students preparing for
HKDSE ICT. Many students revise by doing large numbers of questions, but they rarely learn why
an answer was wrong, and their time is not focused on their weakest topics.
The app uses AI in four linked features: an adaptive pre-test finds the starting point; a knowledge
map shows which points are mastered, need review or still need evidence; a daily AI study plan
puts weak points first and fits the exam date and daily study time; and an AI mistake guide asks
the student one question per step instead of showing the answer, so they find the error
themselves. Each guided correction is saved to the map, so tomorrow’s plan changes.
User flow (script). A new user signs up, takes a 10-question pre-test and sees the result; a
returning user logs in. Both land on Today, follow the AI plan or browse Learn, and practise. A
wrong answer opens the mistake guide, which ends with an AI recap and returns to practice. The
tab bar also gives Ask AI, Insights and Me. The app has 12 screens; the same numbers 1–12 are
used in the wireframes, the UI and the storyboard.
2. Evaluation of existing apps
Why these three. Each is a leading example of one way students study with a phone today, and
each has more than 100,000 App Store ratings: an AI tutor on a course platform (Khan Academy),
an AI problem solver (Photomath) and a flashcard and practice app (Quizlet). Together they
cover the three things this app combines: lessons, practice, and AI help with mistakes.
Method. The three apps are rated on the same five criteria (Section 2.4), taken from the needs of
an HKDSE ICT student. Ratings are App Store (US) and Google Play figures from app trackers,
checked on 11 November 2026; prices are in US dollars and may differ in Hong Kong. verify ratings
on the HK App Store when taking screenshots

3. Competitive advantages
No single app covers all five criteria for an HKDSE ICT student. Khanmigo already guides learners
with questions, so guided help alone is not new. The advantage of ICT Learning Companion is that
it joins guided help, gap analysis and exam planning around one syllabus, and makes them
available to Hong Kong students.

4. Wireframes
The wireframes are low fidelity and greyscale, so attention stays on structure and interaction
rather than colour. They follow the user flow in Section 1 and are grouped into the same four parts
as the storyboard: Onboarding (screens 1–3), Daily learning (4–6), AI mistake guide (7–9) and
Other app features (10–12). Page 1 is an overview map of how the 12 screens connect.
Labelling. Every tappable element has a numbered badge, and the note under the screen gives
the element’s name and where it leads, using the button text from the UI (for example, “Guide me
step by step → 8”). Arrows between screens name the action that moves the user on.
Keeping cognitive load low. Each screen has one main action (dark button) near the bottom and
at most one secondary action; back links sit top-left; the five-tab bar stays in the same place;
content that comes from AI always sits in a bold-framed box with an “AI” label, so the learner can
see what the AI produced.

5. UI design
Concept. Revision is stressful, so the interface is calm and paper-like: a warm off-white
background, white cards with soft corners and plenty of space. The design shows only what the
student needs for the next step.
Colour. One rule links colour to meaning: purple marks anything the AI produces (plan, analysis,
hints, recap), green marks learning actions (log in, submit, practise). Amber marks points to
review; red appears only on a wrong answer.
Background #F6F5F1
Learning green #3E6550
AI purple #5B4FA6
AI tint #ECEAF5
Review amber #D9A54A
Mistake red #B4473B
Text #2D3230
Typography and layout. Inter throughout: titles 26–28 px bold, card titles 17 px, body 13–14 px,
labels 11.5 px in capitals. Screens use a 390 × 844 frame with 29 px side margins, a five-tab bar
with Ask AI in the centre, and touch targets at least 40 px high. The AI spark icon appears on
every AI card and AI button.

6. Storyboard with I/O
The storyboard is a flowchart in four bands that match the wireframes. Circles mark start and end
points, diamonds are decisions with YES/NO branches, and each screen card carries a small
screenshot of the UI and a yellow number (1–12). Grey Input tags show what the learner gives the
app; teal Output tags show what the app returns.
Main logic. Have an account? splits returning users (Log in) from new users (Sign up → Pre-test
→ Pre-test result). On Today, Follow AI plan? leads straight to Practice or through Learn and a
knowledge point. In Practice, Answer correct? continues to the next question; a wrong answer
opens the mistake guide, where Step right? gives a hint and another try, and All 3 steps done?
ends in the AI recap and a return to practice. The tab bar leads to Ask AI, Insights and Me, which
ends with Log out?.

8. Limitations and future work
This is a design prototype. The AI analysis, plan and mistake guide are designed but not yet connected
to a live AI service; questions and figures on the screens are examples. AI output can be wrong, so
every AI card has “May be wrong · Report” and the learner can always ask for the answer. Future work:
connect a server-side AI service, add a Traditional Chinese interface, and a teacher view of class-level
weak points.
