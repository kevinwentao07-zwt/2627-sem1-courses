Student Activity 1: Gender Stereotypes  
 
Name of activity: This is how we grew up 
Learning objectives: To discuss how social norms (family and school) shape our sex roles 
through reflection on our own experience of growing up, and to explore how we can break through 
social expectations of our sex roles  
Relevant subjects: Social Studies, Civic Education, Religious & Moral Education, Counselling, 
Life Education, Sex Education, Form Teacher’s Session 
Target students: junior secondary students 
Duration: ~20 mins 
Venue: classroom 
Resources: Worksheet 1 
Procedure: 
1. Ask students to fill in Worksheet 1 anonymously: This is how I grew up (10 mins). 
2. Collect all worksheets; pick a few copies (including both boys and girls) to compare the 
differences; ask students these questions: 
• In the process of growing up, what are the expected behavior differences between boys and 
girls? 
• How do these expected behavior differences affect the growth of boys and girls? 
• Do these expected behavior differences have to exist?  Are there any ways for breakthrough? 
Note: The teacher can give some daily life examples to reconsider these stereotypes e.g., girls are 
not encouraged to play football; boys are not encouraged to play with dolls or to like the colour of 
pink. 
Conclusion:  
1. Some gender differences may look part of our nature but they may not really be inborn but 
rather are shaped by nurture. 
2. The environment in which we grow up, in both our family and school, is shaping our sex roles.  
Yet these sex roles can be broken through as long as ourselves, our family and school are 
willing to change.  There can be more room for the development of both sexes if we can give 
up our preconceptions about stereotypical sex roles.

## 第 2 页

Worksheet 1: This is how we grew up 
Recall, from birth to now, how you have been expected to play your role of ‘I am a boy’ or ‘I am 
a girl’ by answering these questions: 
 
1. What toys and games did I like most 
when I was young?   
 
 
 
2. What kind and colour of clothes did I 
have on when I was young? 
 
 
 
3. At what age did I start to realise that boys 
and girls are different? 
 
 
 
4. When I got to secondary school, what 
extra-curricular activities do I join? 
 
 
 
5. In a class with boys and girls, is it boys 
or girls that teachers usually ask to help 
carry heavy things e.g., books? 
 
 
 
6. What kind of language do teachers use to 
tell off boys / girls when they do 
something wrong? How is the language 
different?  
 
 
 
7. My parents / teachers always tell me, 
‘You are a boy / girl, so you have to …’  
 
 
 
8. If I were a boy/girl, I would like to …
