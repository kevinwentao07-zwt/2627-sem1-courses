Priority Values and Attitudes to be Cultivated in Students
In response to students’ developmental needs and changes in the social environment, the Education Bureau continues to refine the priority values.
Traditional Chinese virtues are the essence of Chinese culture and form the foundation of the priority values and attitudes. These values are also compatible with the socialist core values advocated by the country. They provide clear directions for curriculum development and learning focuses for the cross-curricular areas under values education. Through connecting learning with Chinese culture, students are enabled to understand and identify with the significance of cultivating the priority values and attitudes, and to put them into practice.
Based on their school missions, visions, educational philosophies, distinctive characteristics, school contexts and religious backgrounds, schools should organically integrate the priority values into their education. Schools should also appropriately select or incorporate school-based positive values and attitudes according to students’ developmental characteristics and learning needs at different stages, such as politeness, integrity, gratitude, cherishing and peace.
In addition, different Key Learning Areas, subjects and cross-curricular areas also have their relevant values and attitudes, such as benevolence, tolerance, frugality and generosity. Teachers may flexibly connect different learning experiences and whole-person development activities inside and outside the classroom, and integrate different values with one another.
The meanings and significance of the 
twelve priority values
 are as follows:
Perseverance
Cultivate in students a spirit of perseverance, encouraging them to set goals for the future and strive actively towards them, while maintaining positive intentions throughout the process, having the courage to overcome challenges, turning challenges into opportunities, and persevering to the end. Students should understand that success depends on sustained commitment and diligent effort, and be willing to endure hardship and work hard. Even when facing adversity and difficulties, they should not give up easily, but demonstrate self-reliance, resilience and the courage to confront difficulties, while approaching life and learning with a serious and positive attitude. A spirit of perseverance helps students achieve their goals and motivates them to make unremitting efforts. At all times and in all circumstances, they should uphold the will to strive for improvement, contribute to society, and put their aspirations into practice without slackening.
Respect for Others
Respect is a fundamental element of interpersonal relationships. Cultivating respect for others among students can strengthen their ability to communicate and cooperate with others and promote harmonious interpersonal relationships. Chinese people attach great importance to human relationships. For example, within the family, one should understand one’s role and 
practise
 harmony and mutual respect between husband and wife, parental kindness and filial piety, and fraternal friendship and respect. In friendships, one should “respect others for what makes them different”; this means respecting differences while upholding principles of right and wrong. When friends behave improperly or deviate from the right path, one should “admonish one another 
towards goodness” rather than blindly follow them. At the societal level, students should respect diverse cultures and opinions and 
practise
 the principle of “harmony without uniformity.” Respect also includes respect for oneself, others and life. In the intelligent age, respect is also manifested in handling one’s own and others’ personal data with care, and understanding that everyone’s right to privacy should be respected and protected.
Sense of Responsibility
Cultivate students’ sense of responsibility so that they exercise self-discipline and self-respect in their words and actions. Whether in 
favourable
 or adverse circumstances, they should fulfil their duties, cultivate their moral character, and achieve the ideal of “when poor, cultivate one’s own virtue; when prosperous, contribute to the well-being of all under Heaven.” They should also learn to care for themselves and take responsibility for their personal conduct, studies and life, in accordance with the expectations of their families, schools and society. At different stages and in different circumstances, they should earnestly fulfil their roles, complete their tasks wholeheartedly and proactively, and give their utmost effort, doing things “from beginning to end” rather than perfunctorily. They should consciously take responsibility for the consequences of their words and actions and refrain from doing anything that would bring disrepute to their family, school or country.
National Identity
Hong Kong is an inseparable part of China; a country provides the foundation for the family. Therefore, cultivating students’ national identity is an important mission of the school curriculum and an inherent responsibility of schools. As the country continues to develop, schools should deepen students’ understanding from an early age of the Constitution and the Basic Law, national security education, “One Country, Two Systems” and the country’s conditions, as well as their identification with Chinese culture. Students should understand that the Constitution is the fundamental law of the country and the concept of the supremacy of the Constitution. They should also develop a proper understanding of the country’s history, including its experiences and suffering during wars, thereby gaining a deep appreciation of and cherishing the hard-won peace, and understanding the necessity of safeguarding national security and social stability. Through developing a sense of the nation and cultivating a deep affection for the country and the nation, students can develop a sense of responsibility towards their nation and country, take pride in being Chinese, strengthen their sense of ownership, share the joys and hardships of the country and its people, and understand that “even the humblest person has a responsibility for preserving the realm under Heaven.”
Commitment
Cultivate students’ spirit of commitment so that, having understood their own responsibilities, they are willing to “go a few steps further” and actively consider how to strive for excellence. At the family and school levels, they should take the initiative to care for the needs of family members, actively participate in school services, undertake different roles and responsibilities, and make their best contributions to their families and schools. At the levels of society, the nation and the world, they should actively participate in actions that benefit the community, such as voluntary service and community activities, care about the well-being of the wider public, and learn from the spirit of “being concerned about the worries of the world before others and enjoying its 
joys only after others.” They should contribute their abilities to their families, schools, society, country and the world. Schools should also help students develop the ability to reflect on themselves, the courage to motivate themselves, and the willingness to take responsibility for the consequences of their actions, thereby demonstrating a sense of commitment and accountability.
Integrity
Integrity is an important aspect of personal cultivation as well as the foundation of interpersonal relationships and the rule of law. It encompasses treating people fairly and impartially, conducting oneself with honesty and probity, and being reliable in words and actions so as to earn the trust of others. In cultivating students’ commitment to integrity, at the personal level, students should ensure consistency between their words and actions, “keep their word and follow through on their actions,” refrain from lying and deceiving others, and learn to remain vigilant so as not to be deceived by false information or misleading temptations. At the family and social levels, they should establish relationships of mutual trust, keep their promises, and 
practise
 the principle that “in dealing with friends, one’s words should be trustworthy,” so that others can confidently place their trust in them. At school, they should adopt an honest and conscientious attitude towards learning and eliminate plagiarism. At the societal level, they should uphold the principle of probity and integrity and contribute to building a society in which public duties are faithfully performed and the law is respected.
Benevolence
“
Ren
” (benevolence) is a core value in Chinese culture as well as an aspect of inner moral cultivation. Upholding a heart of benevolence 
emphasises
 self-cultivation and self-reflection: even when alone, one should observe moral and ethical principles as well as social norms. “
Ren
” is also an important element of interpersonal relationships, expressed through concrete actions demonstrating care and concern for other people and for all things in Heaven and on Earth. Cultivating the value of “benevolence” helps students learn to adopt attitudes of tolerance, magnanimity, kindness and acceptance, and 
practise
 the principle that “the benevolent love others.” They should maintain compassion in their hearts and care about their 
neighbours
, disadvantaged groups and the well-being of humanity as a whole, extending this concern to animals and nature, and putting into practice the principle of “loving one’s own kin, extending benevolence to the people, and extending love to all things.”
Law-abidingness
“Without rules, one cannot make squares and circles” and “Law is the standard of the world” both point to the fact that laws and regulations are fundamental to maintaining order. Awareness of and compliance with the law is the foundation of communal life and an expression of civic responsibility. Compliance with laws and regulations is not merely about obeying rules in order to avoid punishment; it is also about safeguarding the interests of society as a whole. Students should understand that breaking the law is not merely an individual matter but can also affect their families, schools and country. Whether at the family, school, societal or national level, and even in the virtual online world, students should uphold principles, exercise self-discipline and 
practise
 careful self-conduct even when alone. They should cultivate an awareness of the rule of law and work together to safeguard the security, prosperity and stability of society and the country.
Empathy
Cultivating students’ empathy means teaching them to uphold the attitude of “do not impose on others what you yourself do not desire.” They should learn to put themselves in others’ positions, extend themselves to others, and consider others as they would themselves, actively placing themselves in others’ circumstances and considering and understanding their situations and feelings. They should appreciate others’ strengths while understanding their shortcomings and difficulties, refrain from 
criticising
 others arbitrarily, and avoid making unreasonable demands of others. They should 
practise
 mutual understanding, mutual accommodation and mutual benefit, embodying the principle that “the sea embraces a hundred rivers because it is vast,” thereby creating a harmonious and friendly atmosphere and building a caring society.
Diligence
“Standing at the top of a hundred-foot pole is not difficult; with diligence, nothing under Heaven is difficult” and “When young and strong, if one does not strive, one will regret it in old age.” Students should cultivate a spirit of diligence, learning and trying persistently without tiring. They should develop an awareness of the value of 
labour
 and cultivate, in daily life, an attitude of not shying away from hardship and striving diligently for improvement. They should enhance their self-care abilities, become accustomed to physical effort and hard work, temper a resilient and unyielding will, and respect workers in all occupations. In their daily lives, they should exercise self-discipline and moderation rather than becoming excessively absorbed in entertainment, and put what they have learned into practice in everyday life.
Filial Piety
“Filial piety is the foundation of virtue.” Chinese communities 
emphasise
 filial piety as the foremost of all virtues. “Filial piety towards parents” is the foundation of personal conduct. From an early age, students should learn to remain mindful of the needs and feelings of their parents and elders, take the initiative to care for them, appreciate the hardship and nurturing they have provided, be grateful for their kindness, remember the teachings of their family, treat their parents and elders well and respectfully, take the initiative to share household responsibilities, maintain family harmony and unity, and understand the importance of 
honouring
 and remembering one’s ancestors. Students should also cherish themselves, live their lives conscientiously and strive for self-improvement so as not to cause their parents and elders worry. “Filial piety and fraternal respect are the foundation of benevolence.” The practice of filial piety should not be confined to the family but should be extended more broadly. Students should willingly serve at school and in the community, actively care for those in need, and promote a culture of respect for the elderly, filial piety and benevolence.
Unity
“Unity” enables small streams to converge into rivers and rivers to merge into the sea. Cultivating students’ spirit of unity enables them to approach matters from the perspective of the well-being of the collective, putting the public interest before personal interests, and demonstrating friendliness, harmony, mutual support and selfless dedication. They should learn to cooperate with one another, work together with one heart and mind, and move towards common goals. Through relevant learning and practice, students should be guided to understand that they are not only members of 
their families but also members of their schools, society, Hong Kong, the country and the broader community of the Chinese nation. They should closely connect their personal development with the future of the country and the nation, embodying the principle that “unity is strength,” and work together for the stability of the country and the sustainable development of society and communities.
The above descriptions of the meanings of the priority values are intended to provide schools with brief explanations for reference. They are not intended to serve as narrow “definitions of terms” or to be mechanically 
memorised
 by students.
The twelve priority values encompass the levels of the 
individual, family, school, social relationships, and society, nation and world
. They run through the content of different cross-curricular areas of values education and constitute principles for self-discipline, interpersonal conduct, action and engagement with the world. They are mutually interconnected. Combining different values can generate greater synergistic effects and is compatible with the socialist core values advocated by the country. For example:
Linking 
“sense of responsibility,” “perseverance” and “diligence”
 can cultivate students’ spirit of perseverance in the face of difficulties and encourage them to devote themselves wholeheartedly to their responsibilities in different positions, thereby demonstrating a spirit of professionalism and dedication.
Linking 
“integrity” and “law-abidingness”
 can further highlight the beliefs in equality, justice, probity and the rule of law.
Incorporating 
“sense of responsibility” and “commitment”
 into 
“national identity”
 can guide students to understand that, as Chinese people, they have a responsibility to protect their country and promote national prosperity, democracy and 
civilisation
.
Teachers may flexibly and vividly integrate the meanings of the various priority values with examples from everyday life and authentic learning contexts, thereby nurturing students’ proper values and attitudes through subtle and sustained influence.
