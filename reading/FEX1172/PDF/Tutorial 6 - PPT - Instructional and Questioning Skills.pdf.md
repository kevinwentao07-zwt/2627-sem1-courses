## 第 2 页

Instructional Skills
Giving Clear Classroom Instructions
Why are clear instructions important?
• Help students understand what they are expected to do. 
• Reduce confusion and unnecessary questions. 
• Save lesson time. 
• Increase students' confid ence and participation. 
• Improve classroom management.

## 第 3 页

Five Principles of Giving Effective Instructions
Good instructions should be:
• Clear – Use simple and familiar language. 
• Concise – Keep instructions short. 
• Logical – Give one step at a time. 
• Specific – Tell students exactly what to do. 
• Visible – Write key instructions on the board or slides whenever 
                      possible.

## 第 4 页

Before Giving Instructions
Before speaking, ask yourself:
• Do students already know the task? 
• What is the purpose of the activity? 
• What information do students really need? 
• Can I demonstrate instead of only explaining? 
• Are any materials ready?

## 第 5 页

During the Instructions
Remember to:
• Gain students' atte ntion first. 
• Speak slowly and clearly. 
• Give instructions in the correct order. 
• Demonstrate if necessary. 
• Pause between important points. 
• Avoid giving too much information at once.

## 第 6 页

After Giving Instructions
Do not simply ask: “Do you understand?”
Instead,
• Ask a student to repeat the instructions. 
• Ask checking questions. 
• Demonstrate the first step. 
• Walk around and monitor students. 
• Clarify misunderstandings immediately.

## 第 7 页

Common Mistakes
Avoid:
• Giving instructions while students are talking. 
• Explaining for too long. 
• Giving several instructions at once. 
• Using complicated language. 
• Beginning the activity before checking understanding.

## 第 8 页

Questioning Skills: Why Do Teachers Ask Questions?
Good questions help students:
• Thi nk deeply. 
• Explain their ideas. 
• Check understanding. 
• Make connections. 
• Participate actively. 
• Develop critical thinking.

## 第 9 页

Characteristics of Good Questions
Good questions are:
• Clear 
• Relevant 
• Open enough to encourage thinking 
• Appropriate for students' level 
• Focused on lesson objectives 
• Able to stimulate discussion

## 第 10 页

Types of Classroom Questions
Closed Questions
• Usually have one correct answer.
• Example: What is the capital of Japan?
• Useful for: checking knowledge; reviewing facts
Open Questions
• Have more than one acceptable answer.
• Example: Why do you think this character acted this way?
• Useful for: discussion; reasoning; creativity

## 第 11 页

Higher-Order Questions
Higher-order questions ask students to
• analyse 
• evaluate 
• justify 
• compare 
• predict 
• create 
instead of simply remembering information.

## 第 15 页

Asking Better Questions
Instead of asking: Is this correct?
Try asking
• Why do you think so? 
• Can you explain your reasoning? 
• What evidence supports your answer? 
• Does anyone have a diffe rent idea? 
• Can someone build on this answer?

## 第 16 页

Wait Time
Afte r asking a question,
Wait at least 3–5 seconds.
Why?
• Students think more deeply. 
• More students participate. 
• Answers become longer. 
• Students become more confid ent.

## 第 17 页

Responding to Students' Answers
Good teachers
•
Listen carefully. 
•
Encourage further explanation. 
•
Ask follow-up questions. 
•
Build on students' ideas. 
•
Appreciate diffe rent viewpoints. 
Avoid saying only
•
"Correct." 
•
"Wrong." 
Instead ask
•
"Can you explain?" 
•
"Why?" 
•
"What makes you think that?"

## 第 18 页

Common Mistakes
Avoid
• Asking several questions at once. 
• Answering your own questions. 
• Calling only on volunteers. 
• Asking only factual questions. 
• Giving students no thinking time. 
• Ignoring incorrect answers.
