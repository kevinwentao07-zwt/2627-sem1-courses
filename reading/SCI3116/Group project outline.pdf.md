Topic Selection Rules 
 
Groups must be formed before selecting a topic. Each group will then have the opportunity to 
choose their preferred topic. If multiple groups select the same topic, priority will be decided by a 
fair method (e.g., rock-paper-scissors), and the winning group will secure the topic. 
 
General Reminders 
1. A reference list is required, and proper citations must be included in both the presentation 
file and the final report. 
2. To demonstrate your critical thinking skills, aim for an in-depth and focused discussion (e.g., 
using case studies) rather than a generic overview. Ensure your arguments are well-
supported by examples and evidence. 
3. Your project must draw from multiple sources. Relying on a single source (even a high-
quality one, such as a documentary) is insufficient for constructing a comprehensive project 
and indicates over-reliance. While you may use one source as a starting point, you must 
expand your research to include others. 
4. Avoid overly generic discussions lacking evidence, as these will result in a low score. 
Conversely, avoid topics that are purely informational and leave no room for critical 
discussion. 
5. For certain topics, the course tutor may provide initial references to help kickstart your 
research. 
 
Group Consultation 
Group consultations will be conducted according to the scheduled times. 
 
Presentation 
Please note the following important reminders when preparing your presentation: 
1. Each group has a maximum of 15 minutes to present (excluding Q&A). Be mindful of your 
slide count and avoid preparing too many. Practice your presentation to ensure it fits within 
the time limit, as the lecturer will stop presentations that run overtime. 
2. Communicate your ideas effectively. Do not simply read the text on your slides word-for-
word (the tutor will have a soft copy to review if necessary). Instead, consider using visual 
aids like diagrams and photos to convey your points. 
3. If you introduce new terminology, you must explain it to the audience. This is essential for 
demonstrating effective presentation skills. 
4. Do not play long, third-party videos. If you use video clips as evidence, limit them to a 
maximum of one minute. The only exception is for videos produced by your group 
specifically for this project, which may be up to 5 minutes long.

5. Manage your time wisely. While providing factual background on the relevant science and 
technology is necessary, ensure you allocate sufficient time to discuss issues that encourage 
audience reflection and judgment (see the "Essay" section below for details). 
6. Cite your sources on the specific slides where the information appears, rather than only at 
the end of the presentation. 
7. Proofread and edit your presentation slides carefully. Eliminate formatting inconsistencies 
(e.g., varying font sizes or colors) and correct any spelling or grammatical errors. Ensure a 
cohesive visual style throughout the presentation by coordinating formatting and fonts with 
your group members. 
Essay 
The essay should be approximately 3,000 to 3,800 words and divided into two main parts: 
1. History and/or technological background 
2. Discussion (incorporating feedback received during the presentation) 
The discussion section—which encompasses your investigation, critical analysis, elaboration, and 
conclusion—is the most critical component and should make up at least 50% of the essay. 
Remember to cite all sources properly. A complete reference list must be included (this does not 
count toward the word limit). The marking rubric for the essay is located on the final page of this 
document.
