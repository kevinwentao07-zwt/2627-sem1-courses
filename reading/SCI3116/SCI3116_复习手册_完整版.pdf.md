## 第 3 页

1　Lecture 1 – Introduction
⭐ 1.1 Scientific Method 流程图（p.6，老师打了 5 星）
Identify the Problem (question) → Gather Data (research) → Hypothesis → Test Hypothesis
(experiment) → Does the new data agree?
No → 回到 Hypothesis：修改或重新提出假设，再测试
Yes → 回到 Test Hypothesis：继续测试。数据一致也不代表"证明"了，理论永远可以被推翻（连 Popper，见
1.6）
Pilot study（预实验）：正式研究之前做的小规模试验，用来检查方法和流程是否可行
考法：MCQ 问"下一步做什么"；数据不一致或一致时怎么办；给你"20 人试两周再做 2000 人"的情境，问这叫什么
→ pilot study。Section B："Describe the steps of the scientific method and explain why it loops back."
⭐ 1.2 Steps to conduct science：苹果例子（p.7，4 星）
1. Question：Why does an apple fall from the tree?
2. Hypothesis：任何两个有质量的物体之间都有吸引力
3. Prediction & Tests：(i) 地球会吸引橙子；(ii) 苹果会吸引橙子
4. Analysis：(i) 阳性 → 地球吸引物体；(ii) 阴性 → 小物体之间好像没有吸引力 → 引出新问题："吸引力是不是
和质量成正比？"
要点：每个 hypothesis 都要推出可以测试的 prediction；阴性结果不是终点，而是引出新问题 → 科学是循环
的。
考法：MCQ 问 test (ii) 阴性之后会怎样 → 引出新问题，不是推翻全部；或者问哪一个才是可以测试的
hypothesis。
⭐ 1.3 Class discussion：自己提一个研究题目（p.8）
很可能变成 Section B 的 short essay："Propose a research topic and design it using the scientific method"。
考前准备好一个自己的例子（见第 5 部分 Q3 模板）。
⭐ 1.4 绿茶例子：完整的研究设计（p.9，5 星）
Observation
流行病学研究发现常喝绿茶的人心血管疾病比较少
Hypothesis
每天喝绿茶可以通过减少炎症、改善血流来降低心血管疾病风险
Experiment
Randomized controlled trial (RCT)：一组每天喝绿茶，一组不喝（control group），在一段时间
里比较两组的发病率
Analysis
差异是否 statistically significant
Conclusion
判断假设是否被支持；如果被支持，可以进一步研究机制和最佳剂量
考法：control group 的作用；不显著 → 不能说假设被支持；没有随机分组（让人自己选要不要喝）→ 两组本来就
可能不同（生活习惯更健康）。

⭐ 1.7 Karl Popper：Science as Method（p.5，Section B "Philosophy of Science"
核心）
Demarcation problem（划界问题）：什么样的理论才算"科学"？
反对 verification / induction：黑天鹅例子。看到 1,000 只白天鹅也证明不了 "All swans are white"，但一只黑
天鹅就能推翻它。→ asymmetry：再多的证据也证明不了理论绝对正确，一个反例就能推翻它。
Falsifiability（可证伪性）：A theory is scientific if it makes predictions that could, in principle, be shown
to be false. ⚠️ 意思是"有可能被推翻"，不是"已经被推翻"。
说法
可证伪？
All metals expand when heated.
✅ 找到一种不膨胀的金属就能推翻

✅ RCT 没有差异就推翻了
Invisible ghosts that cannot be detected exist.
❌ 不管什么结果都无法推翻
Something good or bad may happen this week.（星座）
❌ 太模糊，什么结果都对得上
Conjectures and refutations：提出大胆的假设 → 主动寻找能推翻它的证据 → 经得起考验的理论只是
corroborated（暂时被接受），永远不算"被证明"。
例子：✅ Einstein 广义相对论预测光线弯曲，1919 年 Eddington 在日食时观测验证，这是 risky prediction；
❌ Freud 精神分析什么行为都能解释 → 无法证伪。

## 第 8 页

3　Lecture 3 – Pseudoscience & Ethics
⭐ 3.1 Pseudoscience 的定义（p.2）
"a system of theories, assumptions, and methods erroneously regarded as scientific" – Merriam-
Webster
重点：被误以为是科学，或者假装是科学（p.17："pseudoscience just pretends to be science"）。
例子（p.4、25）：Vitamin O、water memory、telepathic animal communication、free energy generator /
fuel-less engine、astrology & fortune telling、crystal healing、anti-vaccination、ancient aliens。
考法：sample 原题："Which practice is most commonly associated with pseudoscience?" → Astrology（干扰
项：RCT、systematic review、double-blind study）。情境版：Mark 找人用出生时间和行星位置预测事业。
⭐ 3.2 "Free energy" generator（p.8–10）
Perpetual motion machine（永动机）号称不需要输入能量就能一直运转。但能量只会转换，每次转换都有损耗
（friction、heat）→ 一定会停下来。背后原理是能量守恒（conservation of energy）。
情境
关键问题
A：引擎不能用，只是假装能发电
为什么还有人卖？→ 骗钱，解释很简单
B：引擎真的能无中生有地产生能量
为什么电力公司不用？→ 需要很多额外假设
→ A 的解释简单得多，为 Occam's Razor 铺路。
拓展（p.12）：用土壤或植物光合作用发电的电池不是 free energy。它的能量来自阳光 → 光合作用 → 有机物 → 微
生物，符合能量守恒。区别在于能量从哪里来。
考法："Which principle does a fuel-less engine violate?" → conservation of energy；也可能做成 Occam's
Razor 的情境题。
⭐ 3.3 Placebo & Nocebo（p.18–20）：为什么伪科学"有时候好像有效"
Placebo effect（安慰剂效应）：假的治疗（糖丸、蒸馏水、生理盐水）因为病人期望它有效，病情真的有所
改善。越相信，效果越明显。
Nocebo effect（反安慰剂效应）：拉丁文 "I will harm"。本来无害的东西，因为相信它有害，结果真的出现不
适症状。
拓展：Vitamin O、crystal healing 的 testimonials 可以用 placebo 解释 → 所以需要 RCT + placebo group +
double-blind（连 L1）。
考法：糖丸止痛 → placebo；新的通讯塔还没启用，居民就说头痛 → nocebo；⚠️ 陷阱：placebo group 的人被告
知可能有副作用后觉得头晕 → nocebo。
⭐ 3.4 Occam's Razor（p.21–24）
"When you have two competing theories that make exactly the same predictions, the simpler one is
the better."
更准确的理解：需要最少额外假设的解释比较好。

⭐ 7.3 Mendel's Laws（孟德尔定律，p.8–11）（必考，可能出计算）
Law of Segregation（分离定律）：
每个性状由一对基因（gene）决定，一个来自父亲，一个来自母亲
每个基因有不同版本，叫 allele（等位基因），例如身高有高 / 矮，血型有 A / B / O

slide 的比喻：港币 $20 这个"基因"有 3 个 allele：汇丰、渣打、中银

Phenotype（表型） = 看得到的性状（紫 / 白）；Genotype（基因型） = 基因组合（BB / Bb / bb）
Law of Independent Assortment（自由组合定律）：一个性状的遗传和另一个性状互相独立，例如高 / 矮和胖 /

Q1　Describe the steps of the scientific method. Why does the process loop back to testing even when
the data agree with the hypothesis?
Steps: identify the problem → gather data / research → form a hypothesis → test it by experiment → check
whether the new data agree.
If not: revise or replace the hypothesis and test again.
If yes: keep testing, because it is impossible to do all possible tests and new evidence may overturn the
theory. Theories must stay open to falsification (Popper), and science progresses through continuous
falsification and improvement.
Bonus: the atom model (Democritus → Thomson → Rutherford → quarks).
Q2　What is a pilot study? Why might a researcher conduct one before the main experiment?
A small-scale preliminary study carried out before the main study.
It checks whether the methods and procedures are feasible (questionnaire, measurement tools, timing,
participant compliance), so problems can be fixed early, saving time and cost.
Q3　Propose a research question of your own and explain how you would investigate it using the
scientific method.
Question: Does using a phone in the hour before bed reduce sleep quality in university students?
Observation: Many students report sleeping worse after late-night phone use.
Hypothesis: Phone use in the hour before bed reduces sleep quality (must be measurable).
Experiment: Randomly assign students to two groups: no phone in the last hour before bed vs normal use
(control). Measure sleep quality with sleep trackers or questionnaires for two weeks, keeping other
conditions similar.
Analysis: Test whether the difference is statistically significant.
Conclusion: Decide whether the hypothesis is supported. If yes, study the mechanism (e.g. blue light); if
not, revise the hypothesis and test again.
Bonus: run a pilot study first; use a large enough sample.
Q4　A researcher gives 50 volunteers a vitamin pill daily for a month. Most say they "feel healthier", so
she concludes the pill improves health. Identify two weaknesses and suggest improvements.
No control group, so the placebo effect cannot be ruled out → add a placebo group, ideally double-blind.
The outcome is subjective ("feel healthier") → use objective measures (e.g. blood tests, number of sick
days).
Also acceptable: no random assignment; short duration and small sample; no statistical test.

## 第 28 页

Q5　Explain Karl Popper's idea of falsification and how it distinguishes science from pseudoscience.
Karl Popper argued that a theory is scientific only if it is falsifiable, meaning it makes specific predictions that
could be proven wrong by observation or experiment. No amount of supporting evidence can prove a theory
true (e.g. observing many white swans cannot prove all swans are white), but a single counter-example can
disprove it. Therefore, scientists should actively test their theories and try to refute them; theories that
survive testing are accepted only tentatively. For example, Einstein's prediction of light bending was a risky
prediction that could have failed. In contrast, pseudoscience such as astrology makes vague predictions that
fit any outcome and uses excuses when predictions fail, so it cannot be falsified. Non-science (e.g. art,
religion) does not claim to be science at all. Thus, science is built on continuous falsification and
improvement, and no theory is ever "certainly" correct.
Q6　Explain how scientific knowledge can be both "durable" and "subject to change". Give an example.
Subject to change: new evidence can lead scientists to revise or replace ideas (geocentric → heliocentric
model; atom models).
Durable: most changes are refinements rather than total rejections, so well-tested knowledge remains
useful (Newton's laws are still used for bridges and rockets even after relativity).
Not a contradiction: science is evidence-based and open to revision, and that process builds increasingly
reliable knowledge.
Q7　Using the science vs pseudoscience comparison and Occam's Razor, evaluate the claim "Ancient
aliens built the pyramids because it was too difficult for humans."
"Too difficult" is an argument from ignorance: not knowing how something was done does not mean
humans could not do it.
Occam's Razor: the alien theory needs many extra assumptions (aliens exist, travelled to Earth, only
helped build pyramids, left no other evidence), while the human theory is supported by archaeological
evidence (workers' villages, tools, tombs) → prefer the human explanation.
Caution: Occam's Razor is not proof, and it must not be used to support supernatural explanations just
because they sound simple.
Pseudoscience features: selects only favourable "evidence" (cherry picking), no peer review, not
falsifiable.

Q8　In 2006 the IAU reclassified Pluto from a planet to a dwarf planet. Using this case, explain what it
shows about the nature of scientific knowledge.
New evidence (discovery of similar objects such as Eris, which is larger than Pluto) led scientists to revise
the definition of "planet" (orbits the Sun, round, has cleared its orbit). → Scientific ideas are subject to
change.
Pluto itself did not change; only the classification did. Most astronomical knowledge about Pluto
remained valid. → Scientific knowledge is durable.
The decision was made by the scientific community through the IAU, based on evidence and debate rather
than public opinion ("Protest for Pluto"). → scientific enterprise; science is not authoritarian.
Link to Popper: knowledge is tentative and open to revision.
Q9　Explain how Mendel's pea-plant experiments illustrate the steps of the scientific method.
Question / observation: How are traits such as height passed from parents to offspring?
Hypothesis: Each trait is controlled by a pair of factors (alleles), one from each parent; one allele may be
dominant.
Prediction: Crossing pure tall (TT) × pure short (tt) gives all tall offspring (Tt); crossing Tt × Tt gives tall :
short = 3 : 1.
Test: Controlled crosses (pollination by hand), counting large numbers of plants.
Analysis / conclusion: The observed ratios matched the predictions, which supports the law of
segregation. A further question then led to the law of independent assortment (seed colour and shape).
Strength: the prediction was specific and falsifiable. A ratio far from 3 : 1 would have refuted it.
Q10　A company claims its new cooling paint lowers rooftop temperature. Design a fair experiment to
test this claim.
Hypothesis: Roofs coated with the cooling paint have a lower surface temperature than identical roofs
with ordinary paint.
Independent variable: type of paint. Dependent variable: roof surface / indoor temperature (and
electricity used for air-conditioning).
Control group: an identical roof (same material, size, orientation, location) painted with ordinary paint.
Controlled variables: weather, time of measurement, building use, thermometer type.
Measure many times over several weeks; possibly use a pilot study first; repeat on several buildings.
Analysis: check whether the temperature difference is statistically significant. Conclusion: support or
reject the claim; independent testing avoids bias from the company.
