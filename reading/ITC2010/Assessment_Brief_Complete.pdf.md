Assessment Brief 
Group Project and Presentation 40% (within 20 minutes) 
In this project, students in groups of 3 – 4 will  
(i) 
formulate a real-world problem,  
(ii) 
develop a prototype solution using AI or related technologies (such as Metaverse).  
(iii) 
capture the functionalities of their prototype solution in the form of multimedia (such as videos, 
animation),  
(iv) 
further elaborate their work with the class through a live presentation, thus  
(v) 
providing a comprehensive illustration of their proposed solution.  
The presentation will not only focus on the significance of the problem and technical aspects of the solution but 
also emphasize ethical considerations. Outstanding group projects will be selected for sharing as exemplars. 
6-Step Project Development Guide 
Step 1: Define a Problem and Pain Point 
What is the problem? Who faces the problem? Why is it painful or inconvenient? Why is it worth solving? 
Step 2: Think for a Solution 
What could help the user? Does it need a website, chatbot, app, media, or virtual space? Can the idea be simple but 
useful? Can our group build a first version in a short period of time? 
Step 3: List Out a Plan 
A simple project plan should include: 
- Target user 
- Core features of your prototype 
- Content or data needed 
- Platform or tool you plan to use and why 
- Group roles — who does what 
Step 4: Prepare a Prototype and Do Testing 
Build a first version, then test: Does it work? Is it easy to use? Any bugs? What feedback do we get? 
Step 5: Find Limitations 
Be honest and ask: What can our prototype do now? What can it not do yet? What is missing? What would need 
more time, data, money, or skill? 
Being honest about limitations makes your project MORE credible, not less. 
Future improvement  
Step 6: Promote Your Idea 
You need: 
- A good project title 
- Screenshots or visuals 
- A short demo 
- A promo short video 
- A clear presentation

## 第 2 页

Individual Reflection 60% (around 1200 words) 
Task: Students will be asked to write a reflection on 
(i) their personal learning experiences in this course and 
(ii) elaborate on their understanding of concepts covered in the course as well as areas for improvement in their 
learning products. 
Background & Purpose 
This reflection is the primary individual assessment for ITC2010 Digital Literacy, carrying 60% of your course 
grade. The course builds on foundational AI concepts from the prerequisite Artificial Intelligence Literacy 
(ITC1009), advancing into hands-on engagement with AI applications, generative Al tools, and the societal 
implications of digital advancement. This task asks you to consolidate your entire learning journey, not merely to 
summarize what happened, but to think carefully about how your understanding evolved, what challenged your 
thinking, and what you would do differently as a result. Reflective writing moves beyond surface-level description 
into genuine critical analysis. A strong reflection interprets experiences, connects them to course concepts, and 
demonstrates personal intellectual growth. 
Task Description 
Write an individual reflection of approximately 1,200 words covering the following three dimensions. An indicative 
word allocation is provided to help you distribute your effort proportionally. You are not required to follow it 
rigidly, but use it as a guide to avoid over- or under-developing any dimension. 
Dimension 1 – Your Role in the Group Prototype (~300 words) 
Describe the specific part(s) of the group project prototype that you were personally responsible for. Your 
reflection should focus on your individual contribution, not the group's work as a whole. Address the following: 
• What was your key challenge in carrying out your role? 
• How did you work through or overcome it? 
• Looking back, what would you do differently if you were to redo your part of the project, and why? 
Dimension 2 – Your Generative Al Journey (~500 words) 
Trace the evolution of your understanding of generative AI, from your starting point before this course to where 
you are now. Reflect on how this shift has affected your learning habits during the course, and how you anticipate 
it will shape your future work life in your own discipline or profession. Drawing on your hands-on experience with 
tools in this course, discuss one key strength and one key limitation of generative AI that now feels genuinely 
meaningful to you based on your own experience, not just a definition from a textbook. 
Dimension 3 – A Societal Dimension That Matters to You (~200 words) 
You may have already discussed a limitation of generative AI in Dimension 2 – that counts as one societal concern. 
For Dimension 3, go one step further: choose one additional societal dimension of digital advancements from the 
course (such as data privacy, cybersecurity, fairness and equity, transparency, or accountability) that you have not 
yet addressed. Reflect on it – include a real-world example and explain why this dimension resonates with you 
personally and how the course has shaped or challenged your thinking on it. 
Suggested Structure 
You do not need to write in three rigidly separate sections. A coherent, flowing academic essay is preferred. The 
following is a recommended structure: 
• 
Introduction (~100 words): Briefly describe your starting point – your prior expectations, your level of digital 
and AI literacy before the course, and what you hoped to gain.

Main Body (~1,000 words): Address all three dimensions in a logical, flowing manner. Use reflective language
