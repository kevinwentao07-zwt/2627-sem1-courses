Group 1: Facts about ChatGPT
ChatGPT is an advanced language model developed by OpenAI, based on the GPT (Generative Pre-trained Transformer) architecture. It is designed to understand and generate human-like text, making 
it
useful
 for a wide range of applications including customer support, content creation, tutoring, and more. The model is trained on a vast dataset comprising books, articles, websites, and other text sources, enabling it to grasp nuanced language patterns and provide coherent responses.
The first version, GPT-3, was launched in 2020 and featured 175 billion parameters, making it one of the largest and most powerful language models at the time. ChatGPT, specifically, was fine-tuned from GPT-3.5 and later versions, optimized to generate conversational responses that are more engaging and contextually relevant. OpenAI released ChatGPT to the public in November 2022, offering free access with optional subscription plans like ChatGPT Plus, which provides enhanced features and faster response times.
One of ChatGPT's key strengths is its ability to understand context within a conversation, allowing for dynamic and multi-turn interactions. It can perform tasks such as answering questions, drafting emails, writing code, creating stories, and even assisting with language translation. Despite its advanced capabilities, ChatGPT is not infallible and can sometimes produce incorrect or biased outputs, which OpenAI actively works to mitigate through ongoing research and updates.
ChatGPT is accessible via web browsers and integrates with various platforms through APIs, enabling developers to embed its functionalities into their applications. Its development reflects a broader trend toward AI-driven automation and natural language understanding, aiming to make human-computer interactions more intuitive and productive. As AI technology continues to evolve, ChatGPT and similar models are expected to become even more sophisticated, transforming how we communicate and work.

Group 3: Facts about Co-pilot
Co-pilot is an AI-powered coding assistant developed by Microsoft, designed to enhance software development by providing intelligent code suggestions, completions, and automation. The company behind Co-pilot is Microsoft, in collaboration with OpenAI, leveraging cutting-edge AI research to improve developer productivity. Co-pilot integrates deeply with popular development environments such as Visual Studio Code, GitHub, and other code editors, offering real-time assistance as programmers write code.
Microsoft first announced GitHub Copilot in June 2021, marking its official launch. It was initially available as a technical preview before being released more broadly to developers. The tool quickly gained attention for its ability to suggest entire lines or blocks of code, generate functions based on natural language prompts, and assist with debugging, thereby reducing development time and effort.
The underlying machine learning model powering Co-pilot is based on OpenAI's Codex, a descendant of the GPT (Generative Pre-trained Transformer) models. Codex is trained on a vast dataset of publicly available source code from repositories on GitHub, enabling it to understand programming languages and coding patterns across multiple languages like Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, and more. It uses transformer architecture, similar to GPT-3, but is fine-tuned specifically for code generation tasks. This allows Co-pilot to generate contextually relevant code snippets, understand natural language prompts, and assist developers in writing more efficient and error-free code.
Overall, Co-pilot exemplifies how advanced AI models can revolutionize software development by offering intelligent, context-aware coding assistance, making programming faster and more accessible for developers worldwide.

Group 6: Claude.ai 
Claude.ai is an advanced AI language model developed by Anthropic, an AI safety and research company founded in 2021 by former OpenAI researchers, including Dario Amodei. Named after Claude Shannon, a pioneer in information theory, Claude.ai is designed to generate human-like text, assist with various language tasks, and prioritize safety and alignment in its responses. The platform aims to provide a more controllable and trustworthy AI experience by emphasizing ethical considerations and reducing harmful outputs.
Claude.ai was first introduced in 2023 as part of 
Anthropic’s
 broader effort to create safe, reliable AI systems. It is accessible through APIs and used in applications like chatbots, customer support, content creation, and more, offering users a conversational AI that can understand context, answer questions, and perform complex language tasks.
The underlying technology behind Claude.ai is based on transformer architecture, similar to models like GPT-3.
 However, Anthropic employs unique safety-focused training techniques, such as "constitutional AI," which involves training models with explicit safety and ethical guidelines embedded in the learning process. The models are trained on large-scale datasets of text from diverse sources and refined using reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), enabling the model to produce responses aligned with human values and safety protocols.
Overall, Claude.ai represents 
Anthropic’s
 commitment to developing AI that is not only powerful in language understanding but also inherently safer and more aligned with human intentions, aiming to foster more trustworthy AI interactions across various applications.

Teacher:
“Today we are learning about how people preserve food. 
Before we look at the science, can anyone share a way your family or community keeps food fresh for longer?
”
Student A:
“My family dries fish in the sun.”
Teacher:
“That’s a great example. 
Why do you think drying fish helps it last longer?”
Student A:
“Maybe because there is less water.”
Teacher:
“Yes, removing water makes it harder for microorganisms to grow. 
Does anyone else have a different example from home or your culture?
”
Student B:
“My grandmother makes pickled vegetables.”
Teacher:
“Thank you for sharing. 
Pickling is also a traditional method in many cultures. What do you think the vinegar or salt does?”
