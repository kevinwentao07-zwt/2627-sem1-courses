“Free energy” generator
◦ Perpetual motion machine are machine that suppose to run 
continuously without any noticeable energy input.  
◦ Within any machines energy is being converted from one form 
to another.
◦ However as there are always some energy loss during energy 
conversion (usually to friction or as heat), such machine will 
eventually stop.
◦ Historically, any claims to generate “free energy” from such 
machine had been found to be false.

Placebo effects
• Also called the placebo response.
• A remarkable phenomenon in which a placebo -- a fake treatment, an inactive
substance like sugar, distilled water, or saline solution -- can sometimes improve a
patient's condition simply because the person has the expectation that it will be
helpful. Expectation to plays a potent role in the placebo effect. The more a person
believes they are going to benefit from a treatment, the more likely it is that they
will experience a benefit.
• (Source: MedicineNet.com)
◦ Example: Use of vitamin B as placebo for certain disease

Nocebo effects (反安慰劑效應)
◦ Nocebo (Latin for "I will harm") is something that should be ineffective but 
which causes symptoms of ill health. A nocebo effect is an ill effect caused by 
the suggestion or belief that something is harmful.
◦ Source: the Skeptic’s Dictionary

Occam’s Razor
◦ Originally written in Latin, it is cited in many different 
forms.
◦ The most useful statement of the principle for scientists is
“When you have two competing theories that make 
exactly the same predictions, the simpler one is the 
better.“
21

[PAGE 22]
An example of use of Occam’s Razor
◦ There are some people believe that the Egyptian pyramids are not built by 
human, but instead built by aliens. The major argument is that building 
pyramids were “too difficult” for the ancient Egyptians.
◦ Theory A: Pyramids are built by aliens
◦ Theory B: Pyramids are built by humans
◦ Can you list out the conditions under which theory A or B can be right?
22

[PAGE 23]
Precaution for using Occam’s Razor
◦ It is NOT to say the simple solution is ALWAYS the correct one. 
◦ Critical thinking requires carefully assessing the plausibility of your 
explanations with evidence you have.
◦ Simply picking a simple explanation because it sounds easier for you to 
understand can also be problematic. 
◦ (See this version of “three little pigs”)
23

[PAGE 24]
Precaution for using Occam’s Razor 
(II)
◦ Do not simply evoke Occam’s Razor as “proof” for 
supernatural explanations for observed phenomena. (e.g. 
aliens, ghosts, divine intervention,…)
◦ Why?
◦ If someone claims “aliens built pyramids”, you may think it 
sounds simple, but if you work out the necessary conditions 
to be right, you would realize that such claims require 
MORE conditions (not less) than a more realistic 
explanation that does not involve aliens. (link)

TWO BASIC ORIENTATIONS OF 
ETHICS
◦ Judge if an action is ethical or not by considering either 
◦ The Consequences (results) of the action
◦ OR
◦ The Will (reasons) of the action

[PAGE 45]
DIFFERENT ORIENTATION OF 
ETHICS 
◦ Consequentialism
◦ Justify whether an action is right by examining consequences
of the action
◦ Choosing which course of action is the BEST one out of 
the available options.
◦ Depending on whose outcomes are considered more 
important, there are some of the more common 
orientations
◦ Egoism (利己主義)
◦ Utilitarianism (功利主義)
◦ Altruism (利他主義)

[PAGE 46]
DIFFERENT ORIENTATION OF 
ETHICS(II) DEONTOLOGY (道義論)
◦ The theory of deontological reason does not concern itself with the consequences 
of the action but rather with the will of the action. An action is good or bad 
depending on the will inherent in it.
◦ It is about choosing which course of action is the RIGHT one. 
◦ According to deontological theory, an act is considered good if the individual 
committing it had a good reason to do so. This theory has a duty attached to it. 
◦ For example, we know that killing is bad, but if an armed intruder enters your house 
and you kill him or her, your action is good, according to deontologists. You did it 
because you had a duty to protect your family and property.

[PAGE 47]
CODES OF ETHICS
◦ The main domains in which ethics is defined are governed by a particular and 
definitive regiment of rules called “codes of ethics.” 
◦ These rules, guidelines, canons, advisories, or whatever you want to call them, are 
usually followed by members of the respective domains. 
◦ Depending on the domain, ethical codes can take any of the following forms:
1. Principles, which may act as guidelines, references, or bases for some document.
2. Public policies, which may include aspects of acceptable behavior,
norms, and practices of a society or group.
3. Codes of conduct, which may include ethical principles
4. Legal instruments, which enforce good conduct through courts.
